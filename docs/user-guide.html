Start here

This app is a research assistant for Indian stock-market investing. It reads public market data (prices from Yahoo Finance, files from the National Stock Exchange, mutual-fund prices from AMFI) and turns it into facts you can use: how risky something is, what has happened to money invested in the past, how much tax you would owe, and whether an idea actually worked when tested.

Three things it never does:

  • It never buys or sells anything. It is not connected to any broker account that can place orders. Every “trade” you see is a paper trade: pretend money, recorded to see how an idea would have done.
  • It never tells you to buy or sell a particular stock. It gives facts and odds, and you decide.
  • It does not predict whether a price will go up or down. This was tested many ways (from 30 minutes ahead to 3 years ahead, with charts, AI models and news). Nothing did better than a coin flip. The app says so wherever it matters.

The one-paragraph summary

Looking for the YouTube intraday strategy? It is in the Trading strategies tab → “Same stocks every day”, explained here. Looking for the Call / Put prediction? It is in the Options & risk tab, explained here.

The parts of this app you can rely on are about risk, cost, tax and long-term investing: how much a stock usually moves, the real chance an option pays off, what a monthly investment (SIP) in an index fund has done in good and bad periods, which index fund kept the most of its returns, and how to save tax. The parts about short-term trading and up/down guesses are kept in the Research lab as experiments, clearly labelled, so you can see what was tried and why it did not work.

Opening the app

  1. The app runs on the owner’s Mac. It starts by itself when the Mac is switched on and someone logs in, so there is usually nothing to start.
  2. Open a web browser (Safari or Chrome) on that Mac and go to http://127.0.0.1:3000. Bookmark it.
  3. At the top you will see seven tabs: Home My trades My portfolio Investing Options & risk Trading strategies Research lab. Each tab shows a different group of cards. The app remembers the last tab you used, and a grey line under the tabs says what the current tab is for. On the far right, ? Help & guide opens this guide.
  4. Each box on the page is called a card. Every card has a title, a short grey explanation under it, and its own buttons. Results appear inside the card after you press a button. Some cards fill in by themselves when the page opens.

The three trust levels

Every tool in this guide carries one of three labels. They come from the app’s own tests on past data the tools had never seen.

Words you will see

You do not need to learn these all at once. Come back here when a word on screen is unclear.

WordWhat it means
Stock / shareA small piece of ownership in a company, bought and sold on the stock exchange (NSE or BSE in India).
Symbol / tickerThe short code for a stock, such as RELIANCE or TCS. The app adds .NS for NSE (RELIANCE.NS). You can usually just type the company name in “Ask the app”.
NIFTY 50An index: the combined value of India’s 50 largest companies. When people say “the market went up 1%”, they usually mean NIFTY. The app writes it ^NSEI.
SensexA similar index of 30 large companies on the BSE.
Index fund / ETFA mutual fund that simply buys every stock in an index, so you get the market’s return for a very small fee. An ETF is the same thing traded on the exchange like a share.
SIPSystematic Investment Plan: investing a fixed amount every month (for example ₹5,000) automatically.
Lump sumInvesting a big amount once, instead of monthly.
Return / CAGRHow much your money grew. CAGR is the average growth per year, so different periods can be compared.
XIRRYearly return when money went in and out on different dates (like a SIP or your own trades).
Drawdown / worst fallThe biggest drop from a high point before recovering. “Worst fall 30%” means ₹1 lakh would at some point have been worth ₹70,000.
VolatilityHow much a price usually swings. High volatility means bigger daily ups and downs, in either direction.
Option: Call / PutA contract you pay a small amount for. A Call pays off if the price ends above a chosen level (the strike) by the expiry date; a Put pays off if it ends below. Most bought options expire worthless.
PremiumThe price you pay to buy an option. If the option expires worthless, you lose all of it.
BreakevenThe price the stock must reach by expiry for an option to just pay back its premium.
IV (implied volatility)How big a move option prices are expecting. India VIX is the IV of NIFTY options.
IntradayBuying and selling on the same day. Delivery means holding the shares overnight or longer.
Long / ShortLong = buy first, profit if price rises. Short = sell first, profit if price falls (only allowed intraday in the cash market).
Stop-loss / targetPrices decided before entering a trade: exit at the stop to limit the loss, exit at the target to take the profit.
VWAPVolume-weighted average price: the average price paid for a stock today, weighted by how much traded at each price.
Moving average (20 MA)The average of the last 20 prices, used to see the short-term trend.
ATRAverage true range: how much a stock typically moves in one candle (one 5-minute bar, for example). Used to place stops.
CandleOne bar on a price chart showing the open, high, low and close for a period (5 minutes, 1 day…).
Paper tradeA pretend trade recorded by the app with no real money, to test an idea honestly.
BacktestRunning a strategy’s rules on past data to see how it would have done.
Out of sample / unseen daysTesting a rule on data that was not used to create it. The only fair test; the app uses it everywhere.
ChargesBrokerage, STT (securities transaction tax), exchange fees, GST and stamp duty paid on every trade. The app always subtracts them.
P&LProfit and loss.
STCG / LTCGShort-term / long-term capital gains tax. In India, shares held under 1 year: 20% on profit. Over 1 year: 12.5% on profit above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year. Plus 4% cess.

t-stat / “t ≥ 2”A measure of whether a result is real or likely luck. Roughly, 2 or more means “probably not luck”.
Brier scoreA score for probability forecasts. Lower is better. Used to check whether the app’s odds are honest.

Tab: Home

Ask the app

Reliable facts

Type a question in plain English and the app answers using its tested tools. The easiest place to start.

Steps

  1. Click in Your question and type, for example “Tell me about Reliance”.
  2. Press Ask or Enter. Or tap one of the grey example buttons under the box.
  3. Read the answer: a short summary, tables, and a note at the bottom about what the answer cannot tell you.

Questions it understands

  • A stock: “What will happen to TCS?”, “How is SBI doing?”
  • Today’s market: “Which is the best stock to invest today?”
  • Options: “NIFTY call put this week”, “Options on Infosys”
  • Funds: “Which index fund?”
  • The strategy: “How did the strategy do today?”, “Any signal on Trent?”
Good to know: asking “what will happen” gives facts about risk (how much it usually moves, the chance of a big move, recent news), not a prediction of up or down. The app will say clearly that direction cannot be predicted.
Tab: Home

Analyse today

Describes now, not later

One button that scans the NIFTY 50 stocks (plus the strategy’s four stocks) on today’s 5-minute candles and shows which are trending up, which are trending down, and which have no clear trend.

Steps

  1. Open the Home tab.
  2. Press Analyse today. The scan takes 10–20 seconds.
  3. Read the top line, the orange “Will they keep going?” box, then the two tables.

How to read it

  • Top line: whether NIFTY is trending up, down or mixed, and how many stocks are up / down / unclear.
  • Trending up now = the price is above both its 20-candle average and VWAP. Trending down now = below both.
  • Alignment: strong = today’s trend, the daily trend and the morning move or NIFTY all agree; partial = some agree; weak = only today’s trend. Strongest are listed first.
  • Strategy setup: a fresh YouTube-strategy signal with entry, stop and target, or “wait for a pullback”.

Will they keep going?

  • Each row shows how often stocks in the same situation, at the same time of day, were still moving that way at 3:15 PM over the last ~40 trading days.
  • Even strong trends kept going only about 54% of the time, with an average further move smaller than trading costs. That is close to a coin flip.
  • So the list tells you what is happening now. It is not a list of stocks to buy or sell.

Tab: My trades

Plan a trade, Trade journal, Price alerts

Reliable: arithmetic and discipline

Three cards that make trading disciplined and measurable. They do not predict anything. The app never places orders: you trade in your own broker app and record it here.

1 · Plan a trade

  1. Enter the stock, Buy or Short, Intraday or Delivery, the setup, your entry and stop-loss (target optional: blank = twice the risk).
  2. Press Check the plan.
  3. Read: how many shares to buy, the loss if the stop hits and the gain at the target after charges, and the chance today moves as far as your stop.
  4. The rules check shows ✓ or ✗ for: stop on the right side, risk within your %, reward at least 1.5× risk, trades today, daily loss limit, stop not inside normal noise.
  5. After you place the order in your broker, press I placed this trade: save to journal. Tick “Add price alerts” to be notified at the stop and target.

2 · Trade journal

  • Today box: today’s result, trades taken, and a bar towards your daily loss limit. When the limit is reached it turns red, a Mac notification says “Stop trading for today”, and new plans fail the rules check.
  • Open trades: type the price you exited at and press Close. ✕ deletes a trade entered by mistake (press twice).
  • Your scorecard: total after charges, win rate, average win vs loss, profit per trade, worst drawdown, results when you kept vs broke your rules, and results by setup.
  • My rules: set your capital, risk per trade (1% is common), daily loss limit (2% is common) and maximum trades a day.

3 · Price alerts

  • Enter a stock, “rises above” or “falls below”, and a price. Press Add alert.
  • While the market is open the app checks every 5 minutes, including quick spikes between checks, and sends a Mac notification when the price is crossed.
  • Each alert fires once. Closing a trade removes its stop and target alerts.
  • The Mac must be on and awake for alerts to work.
Example: TCS, buy at ₹2,310, stop ₹2,295, capital ₹1 lakh, 1% risk. The plan says buy 66 shares. If the stop hits you lose about ₹1,091 after charges; at the ₹2,340 target you gain about ₹1,878. All six rules are kept. After 30 or more closed trades, your scorecard shows whether your trading actually makes money after charges.
Tab: My portfolio

My portfolio

Reliable

Enter the shares you actually own. The app works out the tax if you sold today, how to use the yearly ₹1.25 lakh tax-free gain, losses that could cancel taxable gains, how much your holdings typically swing, and what past crashes would do to them. Your data stays on this Mac.

Add shares by hand

  1. Fill Symbol (e.g. INFY), Quantity, Buy price (₹) and Buy date.
  2. Press Add purchase. Repeat for every purchase (one row per purchase, even of the same stock).
  3. If you already sold shares this financial year, enter the gains in the “booked” boxes and press Save booked gains.
  4. Press Refresh analysis.

Or import from your broker

  1. Download your tradebook as a CSV file. Zerodha: Console → Reports → Tradebook → equity, all years. Groww: Stocks → Order history → download.
  2. Open Import from your broker, choose the file, pick “Replace” or “Add to”, press Import.
  3. Export your full history: sales need their original purchases for the tax to be exact.

Did my trading beat NIFTY?

  1. After importing a tradebook, press Check.
  2. The app pretends every rupee you put into a stock went into NIFTY on the same day instead, and compares.
  3. Green text: your choices beat simply owning the index. Red: the index would have done better. “Score vs NIFTY” above 1.00 means you beat it.
Example: you bought 10 Infosys shares at ₹1,500 two years ago and they are now ₹1,700. The card shows a long-term gain of ₹2,000, that it falls inside the ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption so the tax would be ₹0, and how much more of the exemption you could still use this year. This is arithmetic, not advice; check with a tax professional before acting.
Tab: Investing

What to expect

Reliable

What really happened to money left in an index for 3, 5, 10 or 15 years, for every possible starting month since 1997: the worst, typical and best results, and the deepest fall you would have had to sit through.

Steps

  1. Enter a Monthly SIP (₹) amount and choose the Index (Sensex or NIFTY 50).
  2. Read the table row for the number of years you plan to stay invested.

How to read it

  • Worst / median / best: the range of outcomes people actually got.
  • % lost money: how often that holding period ended below what was put in.
  • Deepest fall: the worst temporary drop along the way. Knowing this in advance helps you not panic-sell.
  • Longer periods have far fewer losing cases. That is the main lesson.

Market tilts

Use with care

Compares NSE’s special indices (momentum, quality, low volatility, value, alpha, midcap, smallcap) with NIFTY 50 since 2012, using only data NSE actually published. It shows how much extra each earned per year, and the price paid for it.

Steps

  2. Scroll the table sideways on a small screen.

How to read it

Index-fund picker

Reliable

Every fund that tracks the same index should earn the same. This card shows which ones kept the most, using official daily fund prices.

Steps

  1. Choose the Index: NIFTY 50, NIFTY Next 50 or Sensex.
  2. Read the list, best first.

How to read it

  • Gap to best: how much less a fund earned per year than the best one. That gap is lost to fees and sloppy tracking.
  • Tracking error: how faithfully it follows the index. Lower is better.
  • The top few are usually within a few hundredths of a percent; any of them is fine. ETFs are bought at market price, so check they trade a lot.
Tab: Options & risk

Call / Put prediction (card name: “Live candles → Ling probability”)

Experiment: not better than chance

This is the card that answers “will it go up or down?”. You pick a stock, and it shows three percentages: Call (up), Put (down) and Sideways for the next few minutes, hours or day. It uses recent price candles, an AI model (Ling) and, optionally, news. It sits in the Options & risk tab because it is the most-asked question, but read “What the record shows” before you rely on it.

Steps

  1. Choose a mode at the top: 15‑min only looks at one timeframe; Multi‑horizon mix blends 2 months, 1 month, today, the last hour and the last 15 minutes.
  2. Type a Symbol, for example HDFCBANK, RELIANCE or ^NSEI for NIFTY.
  3. Choose the Interval: 5, 15 or 60 minutes. Smaller intervals describe only the next short stretch (5-minute odds cover roughly the next 20 minutes).
  4. Leave Data source on “Yahoo only” (Groww needs a paid subscription).
  5. Leave “Include news sentiment” ticked if you want news to tilt the odds slightly.
  6. Press Fetch + get probabilities.

How to read it

  • Call % = chance the price ends clearly up. Put % = clearly down. Sideways % = stays within its normal small range. The three add up to 100%.
  • The largest one is the card’s lean. If two are equal it says Sideways / no lean.
  • “Clearly” is measured against the stock’s own usual move (its ATR), so a small wiggle counts as sideways.
  • Typical numbers are around 27% up · 26% down · 46% sideways. Those are simply how often each happens in general.

What the record shows

  • Every prediction is saved and later checked against what really happened. See the Live scorecard box at the bottom of the card, or the Prediction hit-rate card.
  • So far (October 2026) 111 predictions were scored and only about 7% of the leans came true. Its probability scores were worse than a coin flip. Simply guessing “up” every time would have been right 63% of the time. Most of those predictions came before a fix on 9 October, but nothing since shows an edge.
  • Over 31,000 past cases, and in a 14-year test, nothing predicted direction better than the plain average.
So what is it good for? Seeing the normal odds of up, down and sideways for a stock, and watching the live scorecard to judge for yourself. Do not buy a Call because it says “Call 40%”, or a Put because it says “Put 40%”. For deciding whether an option is worth its price, use Call / Put payoff odds below. For whether a big move is likely, use Big-move odds.

Tab: Options & risk

Call / Put payoff odds

Use with care

Before buying an option: what is the real chance it ends above its breakeven by expiry? Based on how much the stock is expected to move, not on guessing direction. When tested, its “80%” happened about 79% of the time.

Steps

  1. Enter the Symbol (e.g. ^NSEI for NIFTY), Type (Call or Put), Strike and Days to expiry.
  2. If your broker shows the option’s price, enter it as Premium; otherwise leave it blank.
  3. Press Calculate odds.

How to read it

  • Chance of ending profitable: often around 1 in 3 for bought options. That is normal.
  • Breakeven: where the price must be at expiry just to get your money back.
  • Use it to avoid overpaying and to size small. It does not say which way the market will go.

Big-move odds

Reliable

The chance that the next day or week moves more than ±1%, ±2% and so on, up or down. The size of moves is predictable (busy periods cluster, results days are known); the direction is not.

Steps

  1. Enter a Symbol and press Show odds.

How to read it

  • Compare each chance with “past year”. If today’s chance is 2× the usual, risk is higher than normal; consider a smaller position or wider stop.

Tab: Trading strategies

Strong-stock swing

Experiment: paper only

A swing-trading strategy (positions held days to weeks) built from a trader interview. The idea: trade only strong stocks, risk a small fixed amount, and let the few big winners pay for the many small losers.

How it works, step by step

  1. Only strong stocks. A stock qualifies if it made an all-time high in the last 12 months and is still within 30% of it. A stock like SBI near its record qualifies; one like Yes Bank, far below an old peak, does not.
  2. Setup A, oversold base: the strong stock has fallen enough that its weekly RSI is 35 or lower, then stops making new lows for at least 5 trading days (a base). Buy on the first daily close above the top of that base.
  3. Setup B, flag: a quick strong rise (at least 4× its normal daily move within 10 days), then a short pause of 3–15 days that keeps most of the gain. Buy on the first close above the top of the rise.
  4. Enter at the next day’s opening price.
  5. Stop-loss 1 ATR (one normal day’s move) below the base or flag low.
  6. No fixed target. A trailing “Chandelier” stop follows the price up: the highest price since entry minus 3 ATR. Exit at the next open after a close below it. Small losses, occasional big winners.
  7. Size each trade to risk 1% of capital, with at most 10 positions open.

Using the card

  1. Open the Trading strategies tab.
  2. Press Scan today's signals (about 20 seconds). It uses completed daily candles only.
  3. Signals list stocks that broke out at the latest close, with the stop and risk. Plan this trade opens My trades with everything filled in.
  4. Watch lists oversold strong stocks still building a base.

What the backtest found (NIFTY 200, 2010–2026, after charges)

  • 788 trades, 37% won, +0.06 R per trade: slightly positive but not statistically reliable.
  • Flag breakouts did best (+0.13 R); oversold-base breakouts about broke even.
  • The trader’s claim holds: the best 10% of trades made 4.5× the total profit; the other 90% lost money together.
  • As a portfolio it made about 6.7% a year (flags only 7.8%) vs NIFTY’s ~10%, but its worst fall was only 7–11%.
  • The stock list is today’s NIFTY 200, which flatters the result.
Should I trade it with real money? Not yet. It is tracked on paper from 14 October 2026; judge it after many months of results.
Tab: Trading strategies

Same stocks every day (the YouTube strategy)

Experiment: paper only

An intraday trading strategy from the Dhan YouTube video “The Power of Trading the Same Stocks Every Day”, coded into the app exactly as the video describes. The app tests it honestly and follows it every day with pretend money.

Where to find it

  1. Open the app and press the Trading strategies tab at the top.
  2. The card called Same stocks every day (subtitle “VWAP + 20 MA pullback · intraday · backtest + live paper”) is the second card in that tab.
  3. Or ask in Ask the app (Home tab): “How did the strategy do today?” or “Any signal on Trent?”.

How the strategy works, step by step

The strategy trades the same four stocks every day: Bajaj Finserv, Trent, Titan and Maruti. It watches their 5-minute charts, so each candle is 5 minutes of trading. The app checks these rules after every candle.

  1. Draw two lines on the chart. The 20 MA, the average of the last 20 candles’ closing prices, shows the short-term trend. VWAP, today’s average price weighted by volume, shows whether buyers or sellers have been in control today.
  2. Decide the direction allowed today. If the price is above both lines, only buy trades (long) are allowed. If it is below both, only short trades (sell first, buy back later) are allowed. If it is between them (mixed), no trade.
  3. Wait for a pullback. In an uptrend, wait for a candle whose low dips down to touch the 20 MA but which closes back above it (and above VWAP). That shows the trend held. In a downtrend, the mirror: a candle whose high touches the 20 MA but closes back below it.
  4. Enter on the next candle. The trade starts at the opening price of the following 5-minute candle.
  5. Set the stop-loss 2 × ATR away from the entry (ATR is how much the stock usually moves in one candle). Below the entry for a buy, above it for a short.
  6. Set the target at twice that distance on the other side: risk ₹1 to make ₹2 (a “1:2” trade).
  7. Size the trade so that hitting the stop loses about ₹1,000 (1% of ₹1 lakh), or 1% of whatever capital you set under “Your size”.
  8. Exit when the price hits the target (profit) or the stop (loss), whichever comes first. Anything still open at 3:00 PM is closed at the market price.
  9. Daily limits: new trades only between 9:30 AM and 1:00 PM, one trade per stock per day, at most 2 trades a day in total.
Worked example, a real signal from 12 October 2026: Bajaj Finserv was below both its 20 MA and VWAP, so only shorts were allowed. At 9:35 a candle rose to touch the 20 MA and closed back below it: a pullback. The app “sold short” 94 shares at the 9:40 open, at ₹1,739.68. Stop-loss: ₹1,750.29 (2 × ATR above). Target: ₹1,718.45 (twice the risk below). If the price fell to the target, the trade would make about ₹2,000 minus charges. If it rose to the stop, it would lose about ₹1,000 plus charges, roughly ₹1,100. If neither happened by 3:00 PM, it would close at whatever the price was then. By midday the price was around ₹1,722–1,725 and the trade showed about +₹1,200 after charges, still open.

What the tests found

  • On about 40 trading days of real 5-minute data, the video rules lost about ₹57–235 per trade after charges (about ₹100 a trade goes to brokerage, taxes and slippage).
  • Only about 38% of past signals ended in profit. A 1:2 strategy needs roughly 38–40% just to break even after costs.
  • Eleven extra filters were tested (limit orders, trailing stops, volume, morning gap, trend day, and others). None was reliably profitable on days it had not seen.
  • Every version lost in the first half of the test period and did better in the second, which suggests market mood mattered more than the rules.
  • The app saves 5-minute data every day, so each week the test covers more days and becomes more reliable.

Using the card

Your size

  1. Enter your Capital and Risk per trade (1% is common) and press Apply.
  2. All amounts on the card are then shown at your size.
  3. Position-size calculator: enter an entry and a stop price and press Calculate. It shows how many shares to buy and what you would really lose at the stop including charges.

Today’s signals

  • Shows the trades each version took today, with entry, stop, target, current profit/loss and status (open or closed). Refreshes every 3 minutes while the market is open.
  • Chance at entry: how often similar past signals ended in profit, about 38%.
  • “Where each stock stands” shows whether each stock is above both lines (only buys allowed), below both (only short sells) or mixed (no trade).

When can we trust it?

  • A progress bar per version towards a finish line set in advance: 100 trades over 40 trading days, profitable after costs with t ≥ 2.
  • Until then it says “too early”. A version losing clearly is marked “does not work”.
  • A Mac notification after each day’s close sums up the day.
Should I trade it with real money? No, not yet. Wait until the trust meter says it passed. If it never passes, the strategy does not work, and you will have lost nothing finding out.

Common tasks, step by step

“I want to start investing every month”

  1. Investing tab → Goal planner: enter your target and years, press Plan. Note the “90% of cases” SIP.
  2. What to expect (same tab): enter that SIP and look at the worst case and deepest fall for your years, so nothing surprises you.
  3. Index-fund picker (same tab): choose NIFTY 50 and pick one of the top few funds (direct plan, growth).
  4. Start the SIP in your own broker or fund app. This app does not invest for you.

“I own shares. What tax would I pay?”

  1. My portfolio tab: import your tradebook or add each purchase.
  2. Press Refresh analysis.
  3. Read “tax if sold today”, the unused part of the ₹1.25 lakh exemption, and the harvesting plan.
  4. Before March 31 each year, check the harvesting suggestions with a tax professional.

“I want to buy a Call or Put option”

  1. Call / Put payoff odds: enter the option and your broker’s price, press Calculate odds.
  2. If the chance of profit is about 1 in 3 or lower, expect to lose the premium most times.
  3. Big-move odds: is a large move more likely than usual right now?
  4. Only spend an amount you are fully prepared to lose.

“Will NIFTY or my stock go up or down today?”

  1. Options & risk tab → Call / Put prediction: enter the symbol and press Fetch + get probabilities.
  2. Read the Call / Put / Sideways percentages, then scroll to the Live scorecard to see how often this card has been right.
  3. Treat the answer as “nobody knows”: the record is not better than chance.
  4. To plan for risk instead, check Big-move odds (is a big move likely?) and, before buying an option, Call / Put payoff odds.

“I want to place a trade safely”

  1. My trades tab → Plan a trade: enter entry and stop, press Check the plan.
  2. Only go ahead if the rules check is all ✓ and you accept the loss at the stop.
  3. Place the order in your broker with that number of shares and a stop-loss order.
  4. Press I placed this trade: save to journal with alerts ticked.
  5. When you exit, close it in the journal. If the daily loss limit is reached, stop for the day.

“I want to follow the YouTube strategy”

  1. Trading strategies tab → Same stocks every day. Set Your size.
  2. Watch Today’s signals during market hours, or ask “How did the strategy do today?”.
  3. Check the When can we trust it? meter each week.
  4. Do not use real money until it says it passed.

“Is my own trading any good?”

  1. Download your full tradebook from your broker.
  2. My portfolio tab → Import, then Did my trading beat NIFTY? → Check.
  3. If the index would have done better, consider putting more into an index fund and less into picking stocks.

“What is happening in the market today?”

  1. Home tab → press Analyse today to see which stocks are trending up or down right now.
  2. Then Today → Refresh for alerts and volatility.
  3. Or Ask the app: “Which is the best stock to invest today?”. You will get a market briefing with facts, not tips.

Golden rules

  1. Never trade real money because of an up/down guess, from this app or anyone else. The app’s own tests show those guesses are no better than chance.
  2. Use the app for risk, cost and tax decisions. How much to invest, which fund, how big a position, how much tax: that is where it is reliable.
  3. Paper-trade any new strategy first, until it has enough trades to judge. The trust meter tells you when.
  4. Only risk what you can afford to lose. 1% of your capital per trade is a common limit, and the position-size calculator does the maths.
  5. Expect bad years. Every investment shown here had long losing stretches. Look at the “deepest fall” before you invest.
  6. This is research and education, not financial advice. For big decisions, speak to a SEBI-registered adviser and a tax professional.

Problems and questions

The page will not open at 127.0.0.1:3000

Wake the Mac and wait a minute; the app restarts by itself. If it still does not open, ask the owner to restart the Mac. The “Today” card will show “Dashboard server is not running” if the scheduled checks found it stopped.

A card shows a red “Error” message

Usually the free data source (Yahoo or NSE) is busy or blocking for a few minutes. Wait and press the button again. A wrong symbol also causes this: use NSE symbols such as RELIANCE or TCS, or type the company name in Ask the app.

It says “OpenRouter has no credits”

The AI writing features (news summaries, the Ask research box) use a paid AI service. Without credits the app uses free backup models, which are slower and sometimes unavailable. Everything else works normally.

Why are option prices or live option data missing?

Live option prices for stocks need a paid Groww Trading API subscription. Without it, enter the premium and IV your broker shows into the card yourself.

The strategy shows “No trades yet”

Paper trades are recorded only after the market closes (after 4:05 PM) on trading days. During the day, see “Today’s signals” instead.

Why does the app keep saying it cannot predict direction?

Because it tested that thoroughly and it is true, for this app and, the evidence suggests, for almost everyone. The app would rather tell you plainly than give you confident numbers that lose money.

Is my portfolio data sent anywhere?

No. Holdings and tradebooks are stored only on this Mac (and in its backups). The app only downloads public market data.

Can the app place an order for me?

No. It has no ability to buy or sell. All trades shown are pretend (paper) trades.
