Live candles → Ling probability EXPERIMENTAL

Two modes: 15‑min only (single timeframe) vs Multi‑horizon mix (2-month 20% · 1-month 20% · today 25% · last hour 20% · last 15 min 15%). Yahoo + Ling. Not investment advice.

What the audit found: these Up / Down / Sideways numbers are honest but are essentially base rates (~27% up, ~26% down, ~46% sideways) — over 31,000 past cases nothing predicted direction better, and "sideways" is measured against the stock's own ATR, so even it can't be forecast beyond its usual rate (14-year test, t −1.3). Intraday is the same: over the next 30–40 minutes these odds did worse than plain base rates, and a model built for 35 minutes lost money after costs (Research lab → Direction over months and years). The 5-minute odds describe only the next ~20 minutes. For the part that is predictable — the chance of a move bigger than ±1% / ±2% — use Big-move odds.

Call / Put payoff odds VOLATILITY-BASED · RESEARCH

The question an option buyer faces: what is the chance this option ends above breakeven at expiry? Answered from the volatility forecast, not from a direction call (the app has no direction skill). Audit: when it says 80% it happened ~79% of the time (within ~1.5 points). The volatility input is the HAR forecast, which beat simply using last year's volatility on 14 years of out-of-sample data (t −2.6) — a small but real edge (~0.5% better scores). Treat the odds as honest, not as a trading signal. Compares the forecast with what the option's own price implies, and its fair value at forecast volatility with the premium. Leave premium blank to price it from implied vol (India VIX for NIFTY, IV you enter, or Groww's chain). Not a recommendation.

Direction over months and years VALUATION · TREND · EVIDENCE TEST

Next-day direction showed no skill, so this tests where finance research finds some predictability: valuation (P/E, P/B, dividend yield) → the next 1–3 years, and the index's own trend → the next 1–3 months. A signal counts only if it is significant (Newey–West t ≥ 2) AND beats the plain historical average when used in real time (out-of-sample R² > 0).

The intraday logic from a trader interview, coded as rules: in the first five minutes (9:15–9:20) find the strongest sector and its strongest stock (for a short: the weakest sector's weakest stock), skip any stock already up or down 5%+ ("don't chase"), enter at about 9:21, stop 1% away, target 1% (only 0.5% when NIFTY's first five minutes are against the trade), and be out by 9:45 whatever happens. NIFTY dip-buy HIGH WIN RATE · INDEX ETF · ~3 TRADES A YEAR · DAILY CLOSES

Buy a NIFTY ETF (NIFTYBEES) at the next open when NIFTY is above its 200-day average but has fallen 3% or more in 5 sessions; sell at the next open after the first close above the 5-day average, or after 10 sessions. No stop: the rising-market filter and the 10-day limit are the protection. About 7 in 10 trades win, but each gain is small and it is in the market only ~4% of the time, so over the years it earns less than simply holding a NIFTY index fund. A second deep-dip version (fallen 5% or more in 10 sessions) is recorded alongside: more of its trades win (about 8 in 10), but it trades only about once a year and earned less per year. Paper only, for learning — not investment advice.

NIFTY short strangle F&O · WEEKLY OPTIONS · PAPER ONLY · RARE BIG LOSSES

Every week, at the close of the first session after expiry, sell one lot of the NIFTY call about 2% above NIFTY and the put about 2% below, and hold both to expiry. If NIFTY stays between the two strikes, both expire worthless and you keep the premium; if it moves past one of them, the loss grows with every point. This was the best-looking of 14 futures & options strategies tested on real NSE prices, and it still did not beat simply holding a NIFTY index fund: it wins most weeks, then one crash week (March 2020) lost about three-quarters of the margin. SEBI found about 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lose money. Recorded on paper only, to watch how it behaves — not investment advice.

The swing-trading logic from a trader interview, coded as rules and tested on today's NIFTY 200 stocks since 2010: trade only structurally strong stocks (an all-time high in the last 12 months and within 30% of it), buy the first breakout after an oversold base (weekly RSI ≤ 35, no new low for 5+ days) or a flag (a quick 4-ATR run, then a 3–15 day pause), stop 1 ATR below the base or flag low, and let winners run with a Chandelier stop (highest high − 3 ATR). Model evaluation REPLAY · CALIBRATION

Replays past bars: at each bar the Call / Put / Sideways model makes its prediction, which is scored against what happened next, side by side with simple baselines. Brier: lower is better (33/33/33 scores 0.667). Skill: improvement over just quoting historical base rates; above 0 means real information. Fit & use stores probabilities fitted to the data; live Call/Put uses them only if they beat the formula on bars they weren't fitted on. Research only — past skill is no promise of future accuracy.
